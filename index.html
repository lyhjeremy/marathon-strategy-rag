A marathon coach that cites its sources

Ask a race-strategy question in plain English. The coach retrieves passages from 14 knowledge cards, answers only from them, and cites each fact as [n]. Six cards summarize my own published analyses of Boston and elite marathon data.

What an answer looks like

RunnerShould I try to negative-split my first marathon?
CoachProbably not as a goal. In 31,912 Boston finishers only 2.5% ran a negative split, and about 94% slowed, median +14% [1]. Aim for an even effort: hold back slightly through halfway so you aren't one of the 27% who slowed by more than 25% late in the race [2].

Illustrative answer assembled from the negative-split and hitting-the-wall cards, showing the citation format. Not a logged transcript.

How it works

  1. Each card is cut into about 120-word chunks with a 30-word overlap and embedded locally with all-MiniLM-L6-v2.
  2. Chunks go into Chroma (cosine distance) with their card, title and source as metadata.
  3. A question is embedded and the five nearest chunks come back with their sources.
  4. Claude answers from those passages only and cites each fact. The CLI is the default backend; ANTHROPIC_API_KEY switches to the API.

The chain is written twice: by hand in coach.py, and as a LangChain LCEL pipeline in chain_lc.py. Context precision of 0.78 is the weak spot; retrieval is dense-only with no reranker. The full writeup has the per-question results and the limitations.
