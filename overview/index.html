A marathon coach that answers only from cited sources

Ask a race-strategy question in plain English, such as "should I try to negative-split my first marathon?", and the coach retrieves passages from 14 knowledge cards, answers from those passages only, and cites each fact as [n]. Six of the cards summarize my own published marathon analyses, so a number like "2.5% of 31,912 Boston finishers ran a negative split" has a repository behind it. On a seven-question LLM-judge evaluation, grounding lifts faithfulness from 0.76 to 0.98.

The pipeline

Five steps from question to cited answer

  1. Chunk. ingest.py splits each card into about 120-word windows with a 30-word overlap, respecting paragraph breaks, and prefixes the card title so every chunk describes itself.
  2. Embed. all-MiniLM-L6-v2 from sentence-transformers (384 dimensions) runs locally on CPU, CUDA or Apple MPS. No API key.
  3. Store. Chroma with cosine distance. Each chunk keeps its card, title and source line as metadata, so citations survive retrieval.
  4. Retrieve. The question is embedded and the top five chunks (-k to change) come back with their sources.
  5. Generate. Claude answers under a system prompt that allows only the retrieved passages and requires a [n] for each fact. It runs on the Claude CLI by default or the Anthropic API when ANTHROPIC_API_KEY is set.

The same chain exists twice. coach.py wires it by hand so the mechanics stay visible; chain_lc.py builds it as a LangChain LCEL pipeline, {context: retrieve | format, query: passthrough} | prompt | llm, selected with --engine lc.

The evaluation

Grounded against bare, question by question

src/eval.py is a small RAGAS-style harness. For each gold question it generates a grounded answer and a bare answer from the same model with no retrieval, then asks Claude as judge for strict JSON scores. Citation validity is computed in code, without the judge.

The biggest gap is the heat question: the bare model scored 0.40, the grounded coach 0.97. The heat card carries a specific number from my Boston weather analysis, about a minute per °F, which a model without retrieval has no way to know. The weakest retrieval is the carbohydrate question, where only 0.40 of the retrieved passages were judged relevant.

The knowledge base

Fourteen cards, six from my own studies

The corpus ships in the repo as markdown, one card per topic, each with a > Source: line. Eight cover established training principles: carbohydrate fueling, carb-loading, tapering, hydration and sodium, even pacing, the long run, race week, and hills. Six are distilled from analyses I ran and published:

Negative splits
2.5% of 31,912 Boston 2014 finishers ran a negative split; about 94% slowed, median +14%.
The wall
Pace inflects at about 30 km; 27% of finishers slowed by more than 25% late in the race.
Heat
About one minute slower per °F of race-day heat at Boston.
Courses
Boston (+101 s) and New York (+78 s) are the slowest Majors against Berlin for an equivalent runner.
Super-shoes
About 67 seconds of the modern elite gain is footwear.
BQ standards
Across 22 age and sex brackets there is no average gender gap, but women's brackets vary three to four times more in difficulty.

Adding knowledge means writing a new card and rerunning python -m src.ingest. There is no retraining.

Limitations

What the numbers don't show

Seven questions is a small gold set, and all seven were written to be answerable from the cards. The scores say the grounded coach is clearly more faithful than the bare model on in-scope questions. They are not a precise measurement, and they say nothing about out-of-scope questions.

The judge is also an LLM, and the same model family wrote the answers. Bare faithfulness measures agreement with the retrieved passages, so a true claim that the cards don't contain counts against the bare model.

Retrieval is plain dense similarity with a single query. A question that spans heat, fueling and pacing would do better with multi-query retrieval, and the 0.78 context precision suggests reranking would help. The sibling wine sommelier RAG uses hybrid BM25 plus a cross-encoder for that reason. Several cards come from single-race Boston analyses, so their caveats travel with them.
